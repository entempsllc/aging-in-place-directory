Aging in Place Contractors in Phoenix, Arizona

10.8% of Phoenix residents are 65 or older, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. Localized leads for accessibility modifications, home care, and heat-safety resources serving Maricopa County.

Desert Climate Planning Note: Phoenix aging-in-place strategies must prioritize heat safety. This includes auditing HVAC systems for extreme reliability, installing solar-reflective window treatments, and ensuring accessibility modifications (like ramps) are constructed with materials that don't reach dangerous surface temperatures. Many local providers schedule assessments in the early morning during summer to ensure senior safety. This page identifies specialists familiar with Arizona's single-story desert home construction and active adult community requirements.

Phoenix's desert climate and single-story home stock make it well-suited for aging-in-place modifications. Most Valley homes are already one level, which eliminates the need for stairlifts but makes bathroom safety, entry ramps, and cooling system reliability the top priorities. Common modifications include walk-in shower conversions, grab bars at toilets and showers, comfort-height toilets, and ADA-compliant ramps for step-down entries.

Help paying for modifications in Arizona

The Area Agency on Aging, Region One is the primary resource for Maricopa County seniors. You may be eligible for the Arizona Long Term Care System (ALTCS), which can fund home and community-based services. Additionally, the Arizona Technology Access Program (AzTAP) provides consultations on assistive devices and home safety equipment.

Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your specific neighborhood in Phoenix or the East/West Valley.
  2. Verify licenses with the Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC).
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, warranty terms, payment schedule, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional, occupational therapist, or other qualified specialist should be involved.

Local help and funding in Phoenix

The Area Agency on Aging, Region 1 (aaaphx.org, 602-264-2227) serves Maricopa County and the Phoenix metro. Contact them to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors. Arizona also has the Arizona Long Term Care System (ALTCS) through AHCCCS, which may cover home and community-based services for eligible beneficiaries.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. You can also call 211 to be connected with local social services and home repair programs.

Phoenix's extreme summer heat makes indoor safety especially critical — confirm that HVAC systems are reliable before summer, and consider installing grab bars in bathrooms adjacent to sleeping areas to minimize mid-day movement on hot surfaces. Verify current eligibility and covered services directly with each program.

How to choose a contractor in Phoenix

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory to find a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in Arizona. If none are nearby, look for a contractor with documented accessible remodeling experience.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check the Arizona Registrar of Contractors at roc.az.gov and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total. Ask each contractor about permits, waterproofing, warranty, and who handles what.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients who had similar modifications done. An online rating alone does not prove accessibility experience.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. If mobility needs are complex, an occupational therapist can assess the home and recommend a prioritized modification plan before you hire a contractor. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — Phoenix

What does it cost to install grab bars in Phoenix?

Professional grab bar installation in Phoenix costs $200 to $350 per bar, with Arizona labor rates of $85 to $300 per project according to HomeAdvisor (2026).

Does Arizona offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The Area Agency on Aging, Region 1 (aaaphx.org, 602-264-2227) serves Maricopa County and may offer home modification or minor repair programs. Arizona also has a State Home Modification Program through the Department of Economic Security.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Phoenix?

A tub-to-shower conversion typically requires a plumbing permit from the City of Phoenix. Permit fees run $250 to $500. Your contractor should pull the permit.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in Phoenix?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select Arizona and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through the Arizona Registrar of Contractors at azroc.gov.

Handyman services for seniors: what they do and what they cost

Handyman services for the elderly cover small home modifications like grab bar installation, door lever swaps, and safety repairs. Most jobs cost $100 to $400 per visit, and a good handyman can handle most aging-in-place projects that do not require a permit or structural work.

By the Aging Gracefully Editorial Team · Published October 12, 2026

How to use this guide: This is general education, not individualized medical, legal, financial, or safety advice. Confirm coverage, eligibility, and product choices with the relevant plan, agency, or qualified professional.

What a handyman does for aging-in-place

A handyman handles small repairs and installations that do not require a specialized trade license or a building permit. For aging-in-place, that typically includes installing grab bars, swapping round door knobs for lever handles, adding non-slip strips to stairs, replacing a standard toilet with a comfort-height model, installing a handheld showerhead, repairing loose handrails, and adjusting doors that stick or are too narrow for a walker.

Most handymen charge $50 to $125 per hour with a two-hour minimum, according to HomeAdvisor (2026). For small jobs like grab bar installation, most quote a flat fee of $150 to $400 per bathroom rather than billing by the hour, because the trip and the minimum service charge make up most of the cost. Installing several bars or modifications in one visit lowers the per-item cost significantly.

Who does the work: handyman vs CAPS contractor vs occupational therapist

Three types of professionals handle aging-in-place home modifications, and they serve different needs:

  • Handyman: Best for individual small jobs — grab bars, lever handles, toilet swaps, handheld showerheads, minor repairs. Typical cost: $50 to $125 per hour, or $150 to $400 per bathroom for grab bars. No specialized training required, but ask for references from senior clients.
  • Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist (CAPS): A contractor or designer who has completed NAHB coursework in accessibility and universal design. Best for whole-home assessments, bathroom remodels, ramp design, and projects that require coordinated planning. A CAPS consultation runs $150 to $400. Learn how to find and verify a CAPS professional.
  • Occupational therapist (OT): A licensed clinician who assesses how a person interacts with their home environment and recommends modifications matched to their specific abilities. Best when mobility needs are complex or changing. An OT home assessment costs $150 to $400 and may be covered by Medicare or insurance when medically necessary.

For a single grab bar, a handyman is usually sufficient. For a full bathroom remodel or a ramp that needs a permit, start with a CAPS contractor or an accessibility specialist. If mobility is changing rapidly or recovery from surgery is involved, an OT assessment before hiring a contractor ensures the modifications match the person's actual needs.

How to choose a handyman for aging-in-place work

  1. Ask about aging-in-place experience. Has the handyman installed grab bars into studs or blocking before? A grab bar anchored only to drywall can fail under a 250-pound load. Ask how they locate studs and what they do when no stud is behind the desired location.
  2. Verify insurance and licensing. Confirm the handyman carries liability insurance. Some states require a contractor license for jobs above a certain dollar threshold — check your state's contractor license lookup.
  3. Request a written, itemized quote. The quote should list labor, materials, fasteners, any trip charge, and whether patching and painting are included.
  4. Check references from senior clients. Ask for two or three past clients in similar situations. A high online rating alone does not prove relevant experience with accessibility work.
  5. Ask when a specialist is needed. A good handyman will tell you when a job is beyond their scope — a curved stairlift, a load-bearing doorway widening, or a bathroom remodel should go to a licensed contractor or CAPS specialist.

Funding help for handyman and modification costs

Most aging-in-place handyman work is paid out of pocket, but several programs may help eligible households:

  • VA HISA grant: The VA's Home Improvement and Structural Alterations (HISA) grant helps eligible veterans with service-connected or certain non-service-connected disabilities pay for home modifications. Check VA eligibility.
  • Medicaid HCBS waivers: Home- and Community-Based Services waivers may cover home modifications that enable a person to remain at home instead of entering a facility. Eligibility and covered modifications vary by state. Learn about Medicaid HCBS.
  • Area Agency on Aging: Your local AAA may offer minor home repair programs, volunteer handyman services, or grants for safety modifications. Find your AAA at the Eldercare Locator.
  • USDA Section 504: The USDA Rural Development Section 504 Home Repair program provides loans and grants to very-low-income rural homeowners for repairs and accessibility modifications. Check USDA Section 504 eligibility.

Verify current eligibility rules and covered services directly with the program before starting work.

Bundling jobs to save on trip charges

Because most handymen charge a two-hour minimum and a trip charge, the first hour of any visit is the most expensive. The practical move is to batch every small modification into one visit: install all grab bars at once, swap every door knob in the same trip, and add the handheld showerhead while the plumber is already there for the toilet. A single visit that installs four grab bars, a lever handle, and a handheld showerhead might cost $400 to $600 total — less than the cost of three separate $200 service calls. Walk through the home with the handyman before they start and point to every item, so they can quote the full list in one written estimate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a handyman charge to install grab bars?

Professional grab bar installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, with a $300 average, according to Angi and HomeAdvisor (2026). Handymen typically charge $50 to $125 per hour, and most apply a two-hour minimum for small jobs. Installing several bars in one visit lowers the per-bar cost.

Can a handyman install a stairlift or wheelchair ramp?

A handyman can install a simple threshold ramp or modular aluminum ramp. A curved stairlift or a permanent concrete or wood ramp requires a licensed contractor, accessibility specialist, or stairlift dealer who handles structural work, electrical connections, and permits.

What is the difference between a handyman and a CAPS contractor?

A handyman handles individual repairs and small installations. A Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist (CAPS) is a contractor or designer who has completed NAHB coursework in accessibility, universal design, and home modification planning. For a single grab bar, a handyman is sufficient. For a whole-home assessment or bathroom remodel, a CAPS professional brings design training that a handyman may not have.

Does Medicare pay for handyman services for seniors?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for home modifications or handyman services. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental home-safety benefits. Medicaid HCBS waivers may cover modifications for eligible beneficiaries. Check your plan's current Evidence of Coverage and your state Medicaid agency.

How do I find a reliable handyman for aging-in-place work?

Ask your Area Agency on Aging for a list of vetted contractors, search Angi or HomeAdvisor for handymen who list aging-in-place or bathroom safety as a specialty, or use the NAHB CAPS directory at nahb.org to find a certified specialist. Always check references from senior clients and verify insurance.

Should I hire a handyman or an occupational therapist first?

For a single grab bar or lever handle swap, a handyman is fine. If mobility needs are complex or changing, an occupational therapist can assess the home and recommend a prioritized modification plan before you hire a contractor. An OT assessment typically costs $150 to $400 and is worth it before a bathroom remodel.

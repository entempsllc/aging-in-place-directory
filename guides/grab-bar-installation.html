Who installs grab bars for seniors, what it costs, and how to choose a pro

Grab bars are installed by handymen, plumbers, accessibility specialists, and CAPS-certified contractors. Installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, and the most important factor is not who you hire — it is whether the bar is anchored into a stud or structural blocking that can hold a 250-pound load.

By the Aging Gracefully Editorial Team · Published October 12, 2026

How to use this guide: This is general education, not individualized medical, legal, financial, or safety advice. Confirm placement, load ratings, and product choices with a qualified professional.

What grab bars are and why anchoring matters

A grab bar is a wall-mounted metal bar rated to support body weight during transfers — sitting down, standing up, stepping into a shower, or catching a fall. The bar itself is inexpensive ($20 to $150 depending on brand and finish), but the installation is the critical part. A grab bar must be anchored into a wood stud, steel blocking, or an approved anchor system rated for at least 250 pounds of force. Drywall anchors — even heavy-duty toggle bolts — will pull out under a sudden load.

According to the ADA Standards for Accessible Design (Section 609), grab bars should be 1.25 to 1.5 inches in diameter, with 1.5 inches of clearance between the bar and the wall, and mounted to support 250 pounds of force in any direction. These are commercial standards, but occupational therapists and accessibility contractors use the same dimensions for residential work.

Who installs grab bars: four types of pros

  • Handyman: The most common and cost-effective option for straightforward installations. Charges $50 to $125 per hour with a two-hour minimum. Best when the bar location lines up with a stud and the wall is drywall. $200 to $350 per bar installed. See our handyman guide for more.
  • Plumber: Needed when the bar is in a tiled shower wall, when plumbing needs to move, or when the installation involves waterproofing. Plumbers charge $75 to $150 per hour. Tile drilling adds $25 to $75 per bar for carbide bits and slower drilling.
  • Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist (CAPS): A contractor with NAHB training in accessibility and universal design. Best when the grab bar is part of a larger bathroom modification or when the person's needs require professional placement judgment. A CAPS consultation runs $150 to $400. Learn how to find a CAPS specialist.
  • Occupational therapist (OT): A licensed clinician who can assess the home and recommend exact placement based on the person's height, grip strength, transfer method, and mobility. An OT home assessment costs $150 to $400 and may be covered by Medicare or insurance when medically necessary. The OT recommends placement; a contractor or handyman does the installation.

How to choose an installer: a checklist

  1. Ask how they find studs and what they do when no stud is present. The answer should involve a stud finder and, if no stud is available, installing 2×8 or 2×10 wood blocking between the studs behind the drywall. If they say "we'll use drywall anchors," find a different installer.
  2. Ask about load rating. The bar and the anchoring system should be rated for at least 250 pounds. Most name-brand bars (Moen, Delta, Kohler) are rated to 500 pounds; the weak link is the anchoring, not the bar.
  3. Ask about tile. If the wall is tile, ask whether they have experience drilling through tile without cracking it. Carbide or diamond bits and slow speeds are required.
  4. Get a written quote. The quote should list the number of bars, locations, labor, materials, and whether patching and painting are included.
  5. Ask about references from senior clients. A good installer will have done this work before and can point to past projects.

Placement: where grab bars go

Standard placement based on the ADA Standards for Accessible Design and common occupational therapy practice:

  • Toilet: One bar on the side wall beside the toilet, 33 to 36 inches from the floor. If the wall is on the right, consider a fold-down bar on the left if no wall exists.
  • Shower or tub entry: One vertical bar at the entry, 18 to 24 inches from the floor to the bottom of the bar, to help with stepping in.
  • Shower back wall: One horizontal bar on the back wall at 33 to 36 inches, plus one on the side wall if the shower is large enough.
  • Tub side: One horizontal bar on the control wall, 33 to 36 inches from the tub floor.

If the person uses a walker, wheelchair, or has had hip surgery, ask an occupational therapist for exact placement — the standard heights may need adjustment based on the person's height and transfer mechanics.

Funding help

  • VA HISA grant: May cover grab bar installation for eligible veterans. Check VA eligibility.
  • Medicaid HCBS waivers: May cover grab bars for eligible beneficiaries. Learn about Medicaid HCBS.
  • Area Agency on Aging: Some AAAs offer free grab bar installation through volunteer programs or grants. Find your AAA.
  • USDA Section 504: Loans and grants for very-low-income rural homeowners. Check eligibility.

Frequently asked questions

Who installs grab bars for seniors?

Handymen, plumbers, accessibility specialists, Certified Aging-in-Place Specialists (CAPS), and sometimes occupational therapists all install grab bars. A handyman is the most cost-effective for a straightforward install ($200 to $350 per bar). A plumber is needed if the bar ties into plumbing or if the wall is behind tile that requires special drilling. A CAPS specialist or OT is best when placement needs to match a specific person's mobility.

How much does it cost to install grab bars?

Professional grab bar installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, with a $300 average, according to Angi and HomeAdvisor (2026). The bar itself costs $20 to $150; most of the cost is labor ($50 to $125 per hour) and the trip charge. Installing multiple bars in one visit lowers the per-bar cost.

Can I install grab bars myself?

You can if you can locate studs or install blocking and have the right tools. The critical requirement is that the bar must anchor into a wood stud or steel blocking — drywall anchors alone will not hold a 250-pound load. If no stud is behind the desired location, you need a professional to install blocking behind the drywall, which involves opening the wall.

What height should grab bars be installed at?

The ADA standard for accessible design (Section 609) places horizontal grab bars at 33 to 36 inches from the finished floor to the top of the bar. The bar diameter should be 1.25 to 1.5 inches, with 1.5 inches of clearance between the bar and the wall. These are commercial standards that occupational therapists also use for residential work.

How many grab bars do I need in a bathroom?

For most bathrooms, plan for at least three: one beside the toilet, one on the entry wall of the shower or tub, and one on the back wall of the shower. If the person uses a walker or wheelchair, add a second toilet-side bar. An occupational therapist can recommend exact placement based on the person's height, grip strength, and transfer method.

Will insurance or Medicare pay for grab bars?

Original Medicare generally does not pay for grab bars because they are a home modification, not durable medical equipment. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental home-safety benefits that may cover them. Medicaid HCBS waivers may cover grab bars for eligible beneficiaries. The VA HISA grant may cover grab bars for eligible veterans. Check your plan and program directly.

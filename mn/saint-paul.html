Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your specific neighborhood in Saint Paul or the surrounding suburbs.
  2. Verify licenses with the Minnesota Department of Labor and Industry (DLI).
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, warranty terms, payment schedule, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work. A high online rating alone is not proof of relevant experience.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional, occupational therapist, or other qualified specialist should be involved.

Official starting points: the FTC home-improvement scam guide, the federal Eldercare Locator, and the NAHB explanation of the CAPS credential.

Local help and funding in Saint Paul

The Minnesota Board on Aging (800-882-6262) serves Minnesota residents. Visit https://mn.gov/board-on-aging/ or call to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. Call 211 for local social services and home repair programs.

Winter safety and aging in place in Saint Paul

Saint Paul's cold winters and older housing stock create specific aging-in-place needs. Entry ramps with handrails are essential for ice season. Bathroom modifications — grab bars ($200 to $350 each), walk-in showers ($1,500 to $15,000), and comfort-height toilets ($350 to $900) — are the most common requests. Saint Paul's historic homes in neighborhoods like Summit Hill and Cathedral Hill often require careful modifications that preserve character while adding accessibility. The Minnesota Board on Aging (800-882-6262) can help identify local programs.

How to choose a contractor in Saint Paul

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory for a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in Minnesota.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check your state's contractor license lookup and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients with similar modifications.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — Saint Paul

What does it cost to install grab bars in Saint Paul?

Professional grab bar installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, with labor at $50 to $125 per hour and a two-hour minimum for most jobs. Installing several bars in one visit lowers the per-bar cost.

Does Minnesota offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The Minnesota Board on Aging (800-882-6262) may offer home modification or minor repair programs. Contact them to ask about eligibility and available services in your area.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in Saint Paul?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select Minnesota and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through your state's licensing board.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Saint Paul?

A tub-to-shower conversion that alters plumbing typically requires a plumbing permit. Permit fees generally run $250 to $500. Your contractor should pull the permit and arrange inspections.

What aging-in-place modifications are most common in Saint Paul?

Bathroom grab bars ($200 to $350 each), walk-in shower conversions ($1,500 to $15,000), and entry ramps with handrails for winter safety. Saint Paul's historic homes often require modifications that preserve character while adding accessibility.

What is the most important aging-in-place modification for a Saint Paul home?

Start with grab bars in the bathroom ($200 to $350 each installed). The bathroom is the highest-risk room for falls. In Saint Paul, where winter ice makes outdoor falls more likely, also add a handrail at the front entry steps. These two modifications together cost under $600 and address the biggest risks.

Help paying for modifications

Coverage and eligibility vary. Original Medicare generally does not pay for permanent home modifications, although it may cover certain prescribed durable medical equipment. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental home-safety benefits; check the plan’s current Evidence of Coverage rather than assuming an item is included. Medicaid home- and community-based services and VA housing grants may help eligible households. Use the federal Eldercare Locator, Medicaid HCBS information, and VA disability housing grants as starting points, then verify current rules with the program.

Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your address and has experience with the specific modification you need.
  2. Verify licenses, registrations, insurance, and permit requirements with the appropriate state or local authority.
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, warranty terms, payment schedule, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work. A high online rating alone is not proof of relevant experience.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional, occupational therapist, or other qualified specialist should be involved.

Official starting points: the FTC home-improvement scam guide, the federal Eldercare Locator, and the NAHB explanation of the CAPS credential.

Frequently asked questions — Philadelphia

How do I screen a contractor serving Philadelphia?

Confirm that the business serves your address and has completed comparable accessibility work. Check required state or local licenses, registrations, insurance, permits, recent references, written warranty terms, and complaint information with the appropriate public authority. A directory listing or online rating is not a substitute for those checks.

What should I ask before a grab-bar installation?

Ask how the installer will identify framing or approved backing, choose fasteners for the wall assembly, protect waterproofing, confirm placement for the user, and handle repairs. Timing depends on the number of bars, wall construction, access, and whether blocking or finish work is required.

How do I compare a walk-in tub and a curbless shower?

Compare transfer method, standing and seated use, caregiver access, fill and drain time, slip resistance, door clearance, waterproofing, plumbing capacity, maintenance, and emergency exit. The right choice depends on the user and the room; consider an occupational-therapy assessment when mobility needs are complex.

Local help and funding in Philadelphia

The Pennsylvania Department of Aging (717-783-1550) serves Pennsylvania residents. Visit https://www.aging.pa.gov/ or call to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. Call 211 for local social services and home repair programs.

How to choose a contractor in Philadelphia

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory for a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in Pennsylvania.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check your state's contractor license lookup and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients with similar modifications.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — Philadelphia

What does it cost to install grab bars in Philadelphia?

Professional grab bar installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, with labor at $50 to $125 per hour and a two-hour minimum for most jobs. Installing several bars in one visit lowers the per-bar cost.

Does Pennsylvania offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The Pennsylvania Department of Aging (717-783-1550) may offer home modification or minor repair programs. Contact them to ask about eligibility and available services in your area.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in Philadelphia?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select Pennsylvania and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through your state's licensing board.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Philadelphia?

A tub-to-shower conversion that alters plumbing typically requires a plumbing permit. Permit fees generally run $250 to $500. Your contractor should pull the permit and arrange inspections.

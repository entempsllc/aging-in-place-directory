Help paying for modifications in Oregon

Oregon residents may qualify for the Oregon Department of Human Services (ODHS) programs. Low-income homeowners in Multnomah County can also check with organizations like REACH Community Development for small home repair and safety programs.

Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your specific neighborhood in Portland or the surrounding suburbs.
  2. Verify licenses with the Oregon Construction Contractors Board (CCB).
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional should be involved.

Local help and funding in Portland

The Oregon Department of Human Services, Office of Aging and People with Disabilities (800-282-8221) serves Oregon residents. Visit https://www.oregon.gov/dhs/apd/ or call to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. Call 211 for local social services and home repair programs.

Wet-climate aging in place in Portland

Portland's rainy climate makes non-slip flooring and entryway modifications especially important for aging in place. Grab bars in bathrooms ($200 to $350 per bar) address the highest fall risk. Tub-to-shower conversions ($1,500 to $15,000) eliminate the need to step over a wet tub wall. Portland's older homes in neighborhoods like Laurelhurst and Irvington often have small bathrooms that benefit from a custom curbless shower. The Oregon Office of Aging and People with Disabilities (800-282-8221) can connect you with local modification programs.

How to choose a contractor in Portland

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory for a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in Oregon.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check your state's contractor license lookup and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients with similar modifications.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — Portland

What does it cost to install grab bars in Portland?

Professional grab bar installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, with labor at $50 to $125 per hour and a two-hour minimum for most jobs. Installing several bars in one visit lowers the per-bar cost.

Does Oregon offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The Oregon Department of Human Services, Office of Aging and People with Disabilities (800-282-8221) may offer home modification or minor repair programs. Contact them to ask about eligibility and available services in your area.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in Portland?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select Oregon and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through your state's licensing board.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Portland?

A tub-to-shower conversion that alters plumbing typically requires a plumbing permit. Permit fees generally run $250 to $500. Your contractor should pull the permit and arrange inspections.

How does Portland's climate affect aging-in-place modifications?

Portland's rainy climate makes non-slip flooring in bathrooms and entryways especially important. Grab bars ($200 to $350 each) address the highest fall risk. Threshold ramps with non-slip surfaces help with wet entries for six or more months of the year.

What is the most important aging-in-place modification for a Portland home?

Install grab bars in the bathroom ($200 to $350 each) as the first step. Portland's wet climate makes non-slip flooring in the bathroom and entry the second priority ($300 to $1,400). A handheld showerhead ($75 to $350) allows seated showering. These three items together cost under $1,000 and address the main risks.

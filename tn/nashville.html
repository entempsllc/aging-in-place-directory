Planning an aging-in-place project in Nashville

Start with the person’s daily routine and the specific barriers in the home—not a product catalog. For bathroom or entry work, ask each provider for a written scope that covers wall blocking or anchors, slip resistance, clearances, drainage, warranties, and who is responsible for permits. For stairs or larger remodels, compare an equipment-only quote with a whole-home accessibility assessment.

Check licensing and permits

First review the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors requirements, then look up the company or individual in the state’s license-verification search. Match the name, license type, status, and monetary limit to the written proposal rather than relying on a logo or verbal claim.

Before work begins, ask Metro Nashville Codes and Building Safety which building or trade permits and inspections apply. Metro states that an owner who occupies or intends to occupy a single-family residence, or a licensed contractor, may obtain a building permit. Put responsibility for applications, fees, inspections, and final sign-off in the contract. A CAPS designation reflects aging-in-place training; it does not replace a required license, insurance, or permit.

Find Tennessee aging, disability, and housing support

Start with the state’s services guide for older adults and adults with physical disabilities. It explains that programs such as OPTIONS for Community Living and other home- and community-based supports are delivered through Tennessee’s Area Agencies on Aging and Disability. Use the Department of Disability and Aging’s official information-and-assistance contacts to ask which agency serves Davidson County and what eligibility rules apply.

For accessible-housing questions, the state’s Housing Innovation program provides a current interest form for housing-related supports. This is a starting point, not a promise that a particular repair or modification will be funded. Confirm eligibility, scope, waitlists, and costs directly before making plans.

Compare Nashville estimates carefully

Prices vary with structure, product, labor, electrical or plumbing changes, permit needs, and finish work. Get at least two itemized estimates, verify insurance, ask for recent comparable references, and avoid paying the entire project cost before work is completed.

Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your address and has experience with the specific modification you need.
  2. Verify licenses, registrations, insurance, and permit requirements with the appropriate state or local authority.
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, warranty terms, payment schedule, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work. A high online rating alone is not proof of relevant experience.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional, occupational therapist, or other qualified specialist should be involved.

Official starting points: the FTC home-improvement scam guide, the federal Eldercare Locator, and the NAHB explanation of the CAPS credential.

Local help and funding in Nashville

The Tennessee Commission on Aging and Adult Services serves Davidson County and the surrounding region. Contact them at https://www.tn.gov/aging/ or call 615-741-2056 to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. You can also call 211 to be connected with local social services and home repair programs.

Verify current eligibility and covered services directly with each program.

How to choose a contractor in Nashville

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory to find a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in Tennessee. If none are nearby, look for a contractor with documented accessible remodeling experience.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check your state's contractor license lookup and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total. Ask each contractor about permits, waterproofing, warranty, and who handles what.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients who had similar modifications done. An online rating alone does not prove accessibility experience.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. If mobility needs are complex, an occupational therapist can assess the home and recommend a prioritized modification plan before you hire a contractor. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — Nashville

How do I screen a contractor serving Nashville?

Confirm that the business serves your address and has completed comparable accessibility work. Check required state or local licenses, registrations, insurance, permits, recent references, written warranty terms, and complaint information with the appropriate public authority. A directory listing or online rating is not a substitute for those checks.

What should I ask before a grab-bar installation?

Ask how the installer will identify framing or approved backing, choose fasteners for the wall assembly, protect waterproofing, confirm placement for the user, and handle repairs. Timing depends on the number of bars, wall construction, access, and whether blocking or finish work is required.

How do I compare a walk-in tub and a curbless shower?

Compare transfer method, standing and seated use, caregiver access, fill and drain time, slip resistance, door clearance, waterproofing, plumbing capacity, maintenance, and emergency exit. The right choice depends on the user and the room; consider an occupational-therapy assessment when mobility needs are complex.

What does it cost to install grab bars in Nashville?

Professional grab bar installation in Nashville costs $200 to $350 per bar, consistent with national rates from Angi (2026). Tennessee labor runs $50 to $125 per hour.

Does Tennessee offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The Tennessee Commission on Aging and Adult Services (tn.gov/aging, 615-741-2056) administers the Older Americans Act programs through regional Area Agencies. Contact the Mid-Cumberland Area Agency on Aging for programs in Davidson County.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Nashville?

A tub-to-shower conversion requires a plumbing permit from the Nashville/Davidson County Building Codes Department. Permit fees run $50 to $300. Your contractor should pull the permit.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in Nashville?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select Tennessee and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through the Tennessee Board for Licensing Contractors at core.tn.gov.

What should I consider for an aging in place bathroom remodel in Nashville?

Start with grab bars at the toilet and shower ($200 to $350 each), then evaluate a tub-to-shower conversion ($1,500 to $15,000). Nashville's older homes in areas like East Nashville and Germantown may have smaller bathrooms that benefit from a custom curbless shower.

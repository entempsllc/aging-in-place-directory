Help paying for modifications

Coverage and eligibility vary. Original Medicare generally does not pay for permanent home modifications, although it may cover certain prescribed durable medical equipment. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer supplemental home-safety benefits; check the plan’s current Evidence of Coverage rather than assuming an item is included. Medicaid home- and community-based services and VA housing grants may help eligible households. Use the federal Eldercare Locator, Medicaid HCBS information, and VA disability housing grants as starting points, then verify current rules with the program.

Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your address and has experience with the specific modification you need.
  2. Verify licenses, registrations, insurance, and permit requirements with the appropriate state or local authority.
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, warranty terms, payment schedule, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work. A high online rating alone is not proof of relevant experience.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional, occupational therapist, or other qualified specialist should be involved.

Official starting points: the FTC home-improvement scam guide, the federal Eldercare Locator, and the NAHB explanation of the CAPS credential.

Local help and funding in San Francisco

The San Francisco Department of Aging and Adult Services serves San Francisco County and the surrounding region. Contact them at https://www.sfhsa.org/aging-adults or call 415-355-6700 to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. You can also call 211 to be connected with local social services and home repair programs.

Verify current eligibility and covered services directly with each program.

How to choose a contractor in San Francisco

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory to find a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in California. If none are nearby, look for a contractor with documented accessible remodeling experience.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check your state's contractor license lookup and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total. Ask each contractor about permits, waterproofing, warranty, and who handles what.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients who had similar modifications done. An online rating alone does not prove accessibility experience.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. If mobility needs are complex, an occupational therapist can assess the home and recommend a prioritized modification plan before you hire a contractor. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — San Francisco

How do I screen a contractor serving San Francisco?

Confirm that the business serves your address and has completed comparable accessibility work. Check required state or local licenses, registrations, insurance, permits, recent references, written warranty terms, and complaint information with the appropriate public authority. A directory listing or online rating is not a substitute for those checks.

What should I ask before a grab-bar installation?

Ask how the installer will identify framing or approved backing, choose fasteners for the wall assembly, protect waterproofing, confirm placement for the user, and handle repairs. Timing depends on the number of bars, wall construction, access, and whether blocking or finish work is required.

How do I compare a walk-in tub and a curbless shower?

Compare transfer method, standing and seated use, caregiver access, fill and drain time, slip resistance, door clearance, waterproofing, plumbing capacity, maintenance, and emergency exit. The right choice depends on the user and the room; consider an occupational-therapy assessment when mobility needs are complex.

What does it cost to install grab bars in San Francisco?

Professional grab bar installation in San Francisco costs $200 to $350 per bar, with California labor rates among the highest in the nation at $90 to $340 per project (HomeAdvisor, 2026). Bay Area rates run toward the upper end.

Does San Francisco offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The San Francisco Department of Aging and Adult Services (sfhsa.org, 415-355-6700) administers programs that may include home modification assistance. California's Medicaid HCBS waivers may also cover modifications for eligible beneficiaries.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in San Francisco?

A tub-to-shower conversion requires permits from the San Francisco Department of Building Inspection. San Francisco has strict building codes; confirm requirements before starting. Permit fees typically run $300 to $500+.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in San Francisco?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select California and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through the California Contractors State License Board at cslb.ca.gov.

Help paying for modifications in Washington State

The Seattle/King County Aging and Disability Services (ADS) is the designated Area Agency on Aging for the region. Washington residents may qualify for the COPES Waiver or other DSHS programs that fund environmental modifications for qualified low-income households.

Five checks before hiring

  1. Confirm the company serves your specific neighborhood in Seattle or King County.
  2. Verify licenses with the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I).
  3. Request a written, itemized scope covering labor, materials, permits, warranty terms, and change orders.
  4. Check recent references for comparable accessibility or aging-in-place work.
  5. For larger accessibility projects, ask who will assess fit and function and whether a CAPS professional, occupational therapist, or other qualified specialist should be involved.

Local help and funding in Seattle

The Washington State Department of Social and Health Services, Aging and Long-Term Care (800-422-3263) serves Washington residents. Visit https://www.dshs.wa.gov/altsa or call to ask about home modification programs, minor repair services, or grant-funded safety modifications for eligible seniors.

Other potential funding sources include the VA HISA grant for eligible veterans, Medicaid HCBS waivers for eligible beneficiaries, and the USDA Section 504 program for very-low-income rural homeowners. Call 211 for local social services and home repair programs.

Aging in place in Seattle's hilly terrain

Seattle's hilly terrain and wet climate create unique aging-in-place challenges. Entry ramps with non-slip surfaces are essential for many homes. Bathroom modifications — grab bars ($200 to $350 each), walk-in showers ($1,500 to $15,000), and non-slip flooring ($300 to $1,400) — address the highest fall risks. Seattle's older homes in neighborhoods like Capitol Hill and Queen Anne often have steep entry stairs that benefit from handrails or ramps. The Washington State DSHS Aging and Long-Term Care office (800-422-3263) can connect you with local programs.

How to choose a contractor in Seattle

  1. Look for a CAPS-certified contractor. Search the NAHB CAPS directory for a Certified Aging-in-Place Specialist in Washington.
  2. Verify licensing and insurance. Check your state's contractor license lookup and confirm liability insurance and workers' comp coverage.
  3. Get three written, itemized quotes. Compare scope, not just total.
  4. Ask about references from senior clients. Two or three past clients with similar modifications.
  5. Consider an OT assessment. See our CAPS specialist guide for more.

Frequently asked questions — Seattle

What does it cost to install grab bars in Seattle?

Professional grab bar installation costs $200 to $350 per bar, with labor at $50 to $125 per hour and a two-hour minimum for most jobs. Installing several bars in one visit lowers the per-bar cost.

Does Washington offer financial help for home modifications for seniors?

The Washington State Department of Social and Health Services, Aging and Long-Term Care (800-422-3263) may offer home modification or minor repair programs. Contact them to ask about eligibility and available services in your area.

How do I find a CAPS-certified contractor in Seattle?

Search the NAHB directory at nahb.org/education/find-an-education-graduate. Select Washington and the CAPS designation. Verify any contractor through your state's licensing board.

Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel in Seattle?

A tub-to-shower conversion that alters plumbing typically requires a plumbing permit. Permit fees generally run $250 to $500. Your contractor should pull the permit and arrange inspections.

How does Seattle's terrain affect aging-in-place modifications?

Seattle's hilly terrain means many homes have steep entry stairs that benefit from handrails, ramps, or stairlifts. Indoor modifications prioritize bathroom safety — grab bars ($200 to $350 each) and walk-in showers ($1,500 to $15,000). Non-slip flooring is essential given the wet climate.

What is the most important aging-in-place modification for a Seattle home?

Install grab bars in the bathroom ($200 to $350 each) as the first priority. Seattle's wet climate makes non-slip flooring ($300 to $1,400) the second priority. If the home has a steep entry, add a handrail or ramp. These modifications together address the main fall risks in Seattle's hilly, wet environment.
